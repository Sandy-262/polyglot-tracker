# Lesson 1: Alphabet & Pronunciation

**Goal:** Recognize the shape of the Malayalam script, understand how it works, and learn the romanization convention used throughout this library.

## Romanization convention used in this library

Malayalam (മലയാളം) is written in its own script, an **abugida** — each consonant letter carries an inherent short "a" vowel unless modified by a vowel sign or combined with another consonant into a conjunct. Throughout these lessons:

- Long vowels are marked with a macron: **ā, ī, ū, ē, ō** (vs. short **a, i, u, e, o**)
- Retroflex consonants (tongue curled back) are marked with a dot-equivalent diacritic: **ṭ, ḍ, ṇ, ḷ, ṣ, ṟ**
- Dental/alveolar consonants (tongue at the teeth) are written plain: **t, d, n, l**
- A trailing **ŭ** marks the short, almost-swallowed closing vowel many words end in (e.g. നല്ല **nallŭ**, "good") — in fast speech it nearly disappears
- **ḻ** represents a sound with no English equivalent at all — a voiced retroflex approximant unique to Malayalam and Tamil (found in the word "Malayalam" itself!)

This is a learning aid, not the official ISO transliteration — don't worry about typing it exactly, focus on the sound.

## Why this language feels different

Unlike Italian, Spanish and French in this library (all closely related Romance languages), Malayalam is **Dravidian** — unrelated to them in grammar or vocabulary. Two big adjustments:
1. The script is new: you're learning to *read* as well as *speak*.
2. Malayalam has an unusually large sound inventory — far more distinct vowels and consonants than English, including multiple "r", "l", "n" and "t/d" sounds that are different phonemes, not accents.

|---|---|---|
| അ | a | short "a" as in "a**bout**" |
| ആ | ā | long "a" as in "f**a**ther" |
| ഇ | i | short "i" as in "b**i**t" |
| ഈ | ī | long "ee" as in "s**ee**" |
| ഉ | u | short "u" as in "p**u**t" |
| ഊ | ū | long "oo" as in "f**oo**d" |
| എ | e | short "e" as in "b**e**t" |
| ഏ | ē | long "ay" as in "s**ay**" |
| ഒ | o | short "o" as in "**o**bey" |
| ഓ | ō | long "o" as in "b**oa**t" |

## Consonants (വ്യഞ്ജനങ്ങൾ) — the tricky contrasts

Malayalam distinguishes dental vs. retroflex consonants — a contrast English doesn't make. Try to feel the difference:

| Malayalam script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ത | ta | dental — tongue tip touches the back of the upper teeth |
| ട | ṭa | retroflex — tongue curls back to touch the roof of the mouth |
| ന | na | dental "n" |
| ണ | ṇa | retroflex "n" |
| ല | la | regular "l" |
| ള | ḷa | retroflex "l", thicker/darker sound |
| ര | ra | light tap, like Spanish single "r" |
| റ | ṟa | trilled/strong "r", like Spanish double "rr" |
| ഴ | ḻa | unique sound, no English equivalent — as in "Malaya**ḻ**am" |
| ശ | śa | soft "sh" |
| ഷ | ṣa | retroflex "sh" |

## Practice

1. Say the 10 vowels above out loud, short vs. long, 3 times each.
2. Practice the dental/retroflex pairs (ta/ṭa, na/ṇa, la/ḷa) back-to-back until you can hear the difference.
3. Record yourself saying "malayāḷam" and compare it to a native recording — focus on the ḻ sound.
4. Read the 10 example words aloud without checking the transliteration, then check yourself.
