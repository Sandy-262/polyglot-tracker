# Lesson 1: Alphabet & Pronunciation

**Goal:** Learn to read French sound-by-sound so new words don't feel like guesswork.

French uses the same 26 letters as English, but several combinations and accents change the sound completely. The good news: once you learn the rules below, French spelling becomes very predictable.

## Accents

| Accent | Name | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| é | accent aigu | closed "ay" sound | été (summer) |
| è / ê | accent grave / circonflexe | open "eh" sound | père (father), tête (head) |
| ç | cédille | makes "c" soft (s sound) even before a/o/u | français |
| ë / ï | tréma | tells you to pronounce vowels separately | Noël |

## Nasal vowels (the signature French sound)

When a vowel is followed by "n" or "m" at the end of a syllable, the vowel becomes nasal (air through the nose, don't pronounce the n/m itself).

| Combination | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| an, am, en, em | nasal "ah" | dans, temps |
| in, im, ain, ein | nasal "eh" | vin, pain |
| on, om | nasal "oh" | bon, nom |
| un, um | nasal "uh" | un, parfum |

## Silent letters

Most final consonants are silent: **petit** (t silent), **beaucoup** (p silent), **vous** (s silent). Final "e" is usually silent too. But **liaison** brings a silent final consonant back to life when the next word starts with a vowel: *les amis* sounds like "lez-ami," *vous avez* sounds like "vooz-avay."

## Tricky letter combinations

| Combination | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ou | "oo" | vous, beaucoup |
| oi | "wah" | moi, trois |
| eau / au | "oh" | eau, chaud |
| ch | "sh" | chat, chocolat |
| gn | "ny" (like Spanish ñ) | montagne |
| ille | "ee-yuh" | fille, famille |
| qu | "k" | qui, quatre |
| r | a soft, throaty sound (back of throat, not rolled) | rouge, rue |

## The French "r"

Unlike English or rolled Spanish "r," the French r is pronounced at the back of the throat, almost like gently gargling. It takes practice — listen to native speakers and imitate rather than overthink the mechanics.

## Practice

1. Say all 10 example words above out loud, then check yourself on Forvo.
2. Record yourself saying "bonjour," "merci," and "aujourd'hui" — compare to a native clip.
3. Pick 3 words with nasal vowels and practice them until the "n"/"m" disappears.
4. Write down 5 new French words you see this week and sound them out using these rules before looking up the pronunciation.
