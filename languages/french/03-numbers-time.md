## Tens to 100 (and the famous French quirk)

| French | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| trente | TRAHNT | 30 |
| quarante | kah-RAHNT | 40 |
| cinquante | san-KAHNT | 50 |
| soixante | swa-SAHNT | 60 |
| soixante-dix | swa-sahnt-DEES | 70 (literally "sixty-ten") |
| quatre-vingts | kahtr-VAN | 80 (literally "four-twenties") |
| quatre-vingt-dix | kahtr-van-DEES | 90 (literally "four-twenty-ten") |
| cent | SAHN | 100 |

French counts 70 as "60+10," 80 as "4×20," and 90 as "4×20+10" — so 95 is *quatre-vingt-quinze* ("four-twenty-fifteen"). Belgian and Swiss French use simpler *septante* (70) and *nonante* (90), but standard French uses the forms above.

## Telling time

| French | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| Quelle heure est-il ? | kel UHR eh-TEEL | What time is it? |
| Il est... heures | eel eh ... UHR | It is ... o'clock |
| ...et demie | ay duh-MEE | ...half past |
| ...et quart | ay KAHR | ...quarter past |
| ...moins le quart | mwan luh KAHR | ...quarter to |
| midi / minuit | mee-DEE / mee-NWEE | noon / midnight |

Example: *Il est trois heures et quart* = "It is a quarter past three."

## Practice

1. Count out loud from 0 to 20, then by tens to 100.
2. Say today's date and the current time in French.
3. Practice 70, 80, 90, and 95 until the pattern feels natural.
4. Say the days of the week in order, then starting from today backward.
