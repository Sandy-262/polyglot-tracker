# Lesson 2: Greetings & Introductions

**Goal:** Greet someone, introduce yourself, and ask basic "how are you" questions — respectfully.

## A note on respect

Kannada (like most Indian languages) has distinct respectful/formal forms, used by default with strangers, elders, and anyone you want to show courtesy to. The polite form of "you" is **ನೀವು (niivu)**; the casual/informal form is **ನೀನು (niinu)**, used only with close friends, children, or much younger people. When in doubt, use **niivu** — it's never wrong to be more formal.

## How are you?

| Kannada | Romanized | English phonetic | English |

| ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? | niivu heegiddiiraa? | NEE-voo hay-gid-DEE-rah | How are you? (formal) |
| ನೀನು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯ? | niinu heegiddiiya? | NEE-noo hay-gid-DEE-yah | How are you? (casual) |

| ನಾನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದಿಂದ ಬಂದಿದ್ದೇನೆ | naanu amerikadinda bandiddeene | NAH-noo uh-meh-ree-kuh-DIN-duh bun-did-DEE-neh | I am from America |
| ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಂದ ಬಂದಿದ್ದೀರಾ? | niivu ellinda bandiddiiraa? | NEE-voo el-LIN-duh bun-did-DEE-rah | Where are you from? |

## Example dialogue

> **A:** ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ! ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ?
> *Namaskaara! Niivu heegiddiiraa?*
> (Hello! How are you?)
>
> **B:** ನಾನು ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ, ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು. ನಿಮ್ಮ ಬಗ್ಗೆ?
> *Naanu chennaagiddiini, dhanyavaadagaLu. Nimma bagge?*
> (I'm fine, thank you. And you?)
>
> **A:** ನಾನೂ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ. ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರೇನು?
> *Naanuu chennaagiddiini. Nimma hesareenu?*
> (I'm fine too. What's your name?)
>
> **B:** ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ರಾಜ್. ನಿಮ್ಮನ್ನು ಭೇಟಿಯಾಗಿ ಸಂತೋಷ.
> *Nanna hesaru Raaj. Nimmannu bheeTiyaagi santoosha.*
> (My name is Raj. Nice to meet you.)

Notice the verb comes last in every sentence ("Niivu heegiddiiraa?" = "You how-are-doing?") — that's the SOV order mentioned in Lesson 1, fully explained in Lesson 7.

## Practice

1. Say the formal greeting and "how are you" out loud 5 times until it feels automatic.
2. Introduce yourself using "nanna hesaru ___" with your real name.
3. Read the full dialogue out loud, playing both parts.
4. Try swapping in "niinu" forms and notice how the sentence changes — but stick to "niivu" until a native speaker tells you it's okay to be casual.
