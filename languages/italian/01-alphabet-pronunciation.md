# Lesson 1: Alphabet & Pronunciation

**Goal:** Learn to read Italian out loud correctly — Italian spelling is far more phonetic than English, so this lesson gets you 80% of the way to sounding right.

## The alphabet

Italian uses 21 letters of the Latin alphabet natively (J, K, W, X, Y only appear in loanwords). Unlike English, almost every letter has **one** consistent sound.

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| a | "ah" | always open, like *father* |
| e | "eh" | can be open or closed, never a diphthong like English "ay" |
| i | "ee" | like *machine* |
| o | "oh" | always a pure vowel, never "ow" |
| u | "oo" | like *moon* |

## The tricky consonants

Italian consonants change sound depending on the vowel that follows — this is the single biggest pronunciation trap for English speakers.

| Letter(s) | Before a/o/u | Before e/i | Example | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| c | hard "k" | soft "ch" | **c**asa / **c**iao | KAH-zah / CHAH-oh | house / hi |
| g | hard "g" | soft "j" | **g**atto / **g**elato | GAHT-toh / jeh-LAH-toh | cat / ice cream |
| ch | always hard "k" | — | **ch**e | keh | what |
| gh | always hard "g" | — | spa**gh**etti | spah-GEHT-tee | spaghetti |
| gn | — | "ny" (like Spanish ñ) | gn**occhi** | NYOH-kee | dumplings |
| gli | — | "ly" (like "million") | fi**gli**o | FEE-lyoh | son |
| sc | hard "sk" | soft "sh" | **sc**uola / **sc**i | SKWOH-lah / shee | school / ski |

## Double consonants matter

Italian is one of the few languages where doubling a consonant actually changes the pronunciation (and often the meaning) — you hold the sound slightly longer.

| Word | Pronunciation guide | English | vs. | Word | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sono | SOH-noh | I am / they are | vs | sonno | SOHN-noh (held "n") | sleep |
| papa | PAH-pah | pope | vs | pappa | PAHP-pah (held "p") | baby food |
| casa | KAH-zah | house | vs | cassa | KAHS-sah (held "s") | cash register |

## Stress (accent)

Most Italian words stress the **second-to-last syllable**: ca-SA, ita-LIA-no, par-LA-re. When the stress falls on the *last* syllable, it's marked with an accent: cit-TÀ (city), caf-FÈ (coffee), perc-HÉ (why).

## Practice

1. Read all 10 words above out loud, slowly, exaggerating the vowels.
2. Record yourself saying "ciao, piacere, grazie" and compare against a Forvo clip.
3. Practice the sono/sonno and casa/cassa pairs — say each pair back-to-back, holding the double consonant noticeably longer.
4. Pick 3 more Italian words you already know (pizza, cappuccino, ravioli) and work out their stress pattern using the rule above.
