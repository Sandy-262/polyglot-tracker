# Lesson 2: Greetings & Introductions

**Goal:** Be able to greet someone, introduce yourself, and have a 30-second first exchange.

## Saying hello and goodbye

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | English | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| ciao | CHAH-oh | hi / bye | informal, friends/family, any time of day |
| buongiorno | bwohn-JOHR-noh | good morning / good day | formal, until mid-afternoon |
| buon pomeriggio | bwohn poh-meh-REE-joh | good afternoon | formal, less common than buongiorno |
| buonasera | bwoh-nah-SEH-rah | good evening | formal, from late afternoon onward |
| buonanotte | bwoh-nah-NOHT-teh | good night | only when leaving / going to bed |
| arrivederci | ah-ree-veh-DEHR-chee | goodbye | formal/neutral |
| a presto | ah PREHS-toh | see you soon | casual |
| a dopo | ah DOH-poh | see you later | casual |

## How are you?

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| Come stai? | KOH-meh STAH-ee | How are you? (informal) |
| Come sta? | KOH-meh stah | How are you? (formal) |
| Sto bene, grazie | stoh BEH-neh, GRAH-tsyeh | I'm well, thanks |
| Non c'è male | nohn cheh MAH-leh | Not bad |
| E tu? / E Lei? | eh too / eh lay | And you? (informal / formal) |

## Introducing yourself

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| Mi chiamo... | mee KYAH-moh | My name is... |
| Come ti chiami? | KOH-meh tee KYAH-mee | What's your name? (informal) |
| Come si chiama? | KOH-meh see KYAH-mah | What's your name? (formal) |
| Piacere | pyah-CHEH-reh | Nice to meet you |
| Piacere mio | pyah-CHEH-reh MEE-oh | The pleasure is mine |
| Sono di... | SOH-noh dee | I'm from... |
| Di dove sei? | dee DOH-veh seh-ee | Where are you from? (informal) |

## Formal vs. informal: *tu* vs *Lei*

Italian, like many European languages, has two ways to say "you":

- **tu** — informal, used with friends, family, people your age, children.
- **Lei** — formal, used with strangers, elders, officials, anyone you'd address with a title. Note: *Lei* takes the same verb form as "she/he" — it feels strange at first but becomes natural.

When in doubt (shopkeepers, hotel staff, someone older), default to **Lei** until they invite you to use *tu* (they might say *"diamoci del tu"* — "let's use tu with each other").

## Example dialogue

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| A: Ciao! Come stai? | A: Hi! How are you? |
| B: Sto bene, grazie. E tu? | B: I'm well, thanks. And you? |
| A: Anch'io, bene! Mi chiamo Marco. | A: Me too, good! My name is Marco. |
| B: Piacere, Marco! Io sono Elena. | B: Nice to meet you, Marco! I'm Elena. |
| A: Piacere mio. Di dove sei? | A: The pleasure is mine. Where are you from? |
| B: Sono di Napoli, e tu? | B: I'm from Naples, and you? |
| A: Io sono di Roma. A presto! | A: I'm from Rome. See you soon! |
| B: Ciao, a dopo! | B: Bye, see you later! |

## Practice

1. Say the full example dialogue out loud, both roles, twice.
2. Introduce yourself in Italian in under 15 seconds: name, where you're from, how you're doing.
3. Practice switching: say "Come stai?" to a friend, then "Come sta?" to your boss — notice the verb change.
4. Record yourself and check your "gli" sound in "Piacere" — wait, that's actually fine, but check your rolled/soft "ch" in "Ciao".
