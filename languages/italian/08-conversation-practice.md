# Lesson 8: Conversation Practice

**Goal:** Put lessons 2–6 together into one flowing, realistic conversation — then practice generating your own variations.

## Full dialogue: meeting, chatting, eating, and finding your way

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| A: Ciao! Come stai? | A: Hi! How are you? |
| B: Sto bene, grazie! Mi chiamo Luca, piacere. | B: I'm well, thanks! My name is Luca, nice to meet you. |
| A: Piacere mio, sono Giulia. Di dove sei? | A: The pleasure is mine, I'm Giulia. Where are you from? |
| B: Sono di Milano. E tu? | B: I'm from Milan. And you? |
| A: Io sono di Firenze. Hai fame? Andiamo a mangiare? | A: I'm from Florence. Are you hungry? Shall we go eat? |
| B: Sì, volentieri! Conosci un buon ristorante? | B: Yes, gladly! Do you know a good restaurant? |
| A: Sì, è vicino, sempre dritto e poi a destra. | A: Yes, it's close, straight ahead and then right. |
| *(at the restaurant)* | |
| Cameriere: Buonasera, cosa prendete? | Waiter: Good evening, what will you have? |
| B: Vorrei una pasta al pomodoro e un'acqua naturale. | B: I'd like pasta with tomato sauce and a still water. |
| A: Per me una pizza margherita, grazie. | A: For me a margherita pizza, thanks. |
| Cameriere: Perfetto, arriva subito. | Waiter: Perfect, coming right up. |
| *(after eating)* | |
| B: Il conto, per favore. | B: The bill, please. |
| A: Grazie mille per oggi, è stato un piacere! | A: Thanks a lot for today, it was a pleasure! |
| B: Anche per me! A presto, Giulia. | B: For me too! See you soon, Giulia. |

## Role-play prompts

Try these out loud, improvising your own words using what you've learned in Lessons 1–7:

1. **First meeting:** You run into someone at a café in Rome. Greet them, introduce yourself, ask where they're from, and ask what they recommend on the menu.
2. **Lost tourist:** You're lost looking for the train station. Stop a stranger, apologize for interrupting, ask for directions, and thank them.
3. **Dinner with a twist:** You're at a restaurant with a friend. One of you is vegetarian and needs to ask about ingredients, and you both need to split the bill at the end.

## What's next

Once this lesson feels comfortable:

- Keep a **daily 10-minute speaking habit** — even just narrating your day in Italian out loud.
- Try **Duolingo** or **Pimsleur** for structured daily practice.
- Find a conversation partner on **Tandem** or **HelloTalk** — real back-and-forth is the fastest way to get comfortable.
- Listen to **"Coffee Break Italian"**-style podcasts for graded listening practice at your level.
- Revisit this lesson weekly — re-read the dialogue out loud and try to improvise new versions of it from memory.

## Practice

1. Read the full dialogue out loud, playing both parts, until it flows without hesitation.
2. Record yourself doing Role-play prompt 1 from memory (no script).
3. Swap one detail in the dialogue (your name, your city, what you order) and redo it.
4. Teach the dialogue's key phrases to someone else (even in English) — explaining it is one of the best ways to cement it.
