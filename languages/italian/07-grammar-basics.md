# Lesson 7: Grammar Basics

**Goal:** Understand the handful of grammar rules that let you build your own sentences instead of just memorizing phrases.

## Sentence structure

Italian word order is close to English: **Subject – Verb – Object**.

> Io mangio la pizza. — I eat the pizza.

Subject pronouns are often **dropped** because the verb ending already tells you who's doing the action:

> Mangio la pizza. — (I) eat the pizza. (still means "I eat the pizza")

## Gender & articles

Every Italian noun is either **masculine** or **feminine** — this affects the article ("the"/"a") you use.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| "the" (singular) | il / lo | la |
| "the" (plural) | i / gli | le |
| "a/an" | un / uno | una |

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| il libro | eel LEE-broh | the book (masc.) |
| la casa | lah KAH-zah | the house (fem.) |
| un ragazzo | oon rah-GAHT-tsoh | a boy |
| una ragazza | OO-nah rah-GAHT-tsah | a girl |

Rule of thumb: nouns ending in **-o** are usually masculine, nouns ending in **-a** are usually feminine (not absolute, but a strong starting pattern).

## Subject pronouns

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| io | EE-oh | I |
| tu | too | you (informal) |
| lui / lei | LOO-ee / lay | he / she |
| Lei | lay | you (formal) |
| noi | noh-ee | we |
| voi | voh-ee | you (plural) |
| loro | LOH-roh | they |

## The verb *essere* (to be) — irregular, memorize it

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| io sono | SOH-noh | I am |
| tu sei | seh-ee | you are |
| lui/lei è | eh | he/she is |
| noi siamo | SYAH-moh | we are |
| voi siete | SYEH-teh | you (pl.) are |
| loro sono | SOH-noh | they are |

## The verb *avere* (to have) — irregular, memorize it

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| io ho | oh | I have |
| tu hai | ah-ee | you have |
| lui/lei ha | ah | he/she has |
| noi abbiamo | ahb-BYAH-moh | we have |
| voi avete | ah-VEH-teh | you (pl.) have |
| loro hanno | AHN-noh | they have |

## A regular -are verb: *parlare* (to speak)

Most Italian verbs fall into 3 families, by their infinitive ending: **-are**, **-ere**, **-ire**. Here's the most common pattern, -are:

| Italian | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| io parlo | PAHR-loh | I speak |
| tu parli | PAHR-lee | you speak |
| lui/lei parla | PAHR-lah | he/she speaks |
| noi parliamo | pahr-LYAH-moh | we speak |
| voi parlate | pahr-LAH-teh | you (pl.) speak |
| loro parlano | PAHR-lah-noh | they speak |

## Example sentences

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Io sono italiano. | I am Italian. |
| Lei ha un libro. | She has a book. |
| Noi parliamo italiano. | We speak Italian. |
| Tu sei stanco. | You are tired. |
| Loro hanno fame. | They are hungry. (lit. "they have hunger") |
