# Lesson 7: Grammar Basics

**Goal:** Understand the handful of grammar rules that let you build your own sentences instead of just memorizing phrases.

## Sentence structure

Spanish follows Subject-Verb-Object, just like English:

> **Yo** (subject) **como** (verb) **manzanas** (object) — "I eat apples"

The subject pronoun is often dropped because the verb ending already tells you who's doing the action: **"Como manzanas"** also just means "I eat apples."

## Subject pronouns

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| yo | I |
| tú | you (informal) |
| él / ella / usted | he / she / you (formal) |
| nosotros/as | we |
| vosotros/as | you all (Spain, informal) |
| ellos/ellas / ustedes | they / you all |

## Gender and articles

Every noun is masculine or feminine — this affects the article used:

| Article | Gender | Example |
|---|---|---|
| el | masculine "the" | el libro (the book) |
| la | feminine "the" | la casa (the house) |
| un | masculine "a" | un perro (a dog) |
| una | feminine "a" | una mesa (a table) |

Rule of thumb: nouns ending in **-o** are usually masculine, nouns ending in **-a** are usually feminine — but there are exceptions (el día, la mano) to learn as you go.

## Ser vs. Estar — both mean "to be"

This is the one concept that trips up every beginner. Use **ser** for things that are permanent/inherent, and **estar** for things that are temporary/a state or location.

| Pronoun | ser | estar |
|---|---|---|
| yo | soy | estoy |
| tú | eres | estás |
| él/ella/usted | es | está |
| nosotros | somos | estamos |
| ellos/ustedes | son | están |

- **Soy** de India — I am from India (permanent identity → ser)
- **Estoy** cansado — I am tired (temporary state → estar)
- **Es** alto — He is tall (inherent trait → ser)
- **Está** en casa — He is at home (location → estar)

## A regular -ar verb: hablar (to speak)

| Pronoun | Conjugation |
|---|---|
| yo | hablo |
| tú | hablas |
| él/ella/usted | habla |
| nosotros | hablamos |
| ellos/ustedes | hablan |

The pattern (-o, -as, -a, -amos, -an) applies to every regular -ar verb: **trabajar** (trabajo, trabajas...), **caminar** (camino, caminas...).

## Example sentences

- Yo soy estudiante y estoy en España. — I am a student and I am [currently] in Spain.
- Ella habla tres idiomas. — She speaks three languages.
- Nosotros estamos felices hoy. — We are happy today.
