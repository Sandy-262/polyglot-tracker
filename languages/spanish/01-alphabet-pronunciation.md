# Lesson 1: Alphabet & Pronunciation

**Goal:** Learn to read Spanish letters the way they actually sound, so you can pronounce any new word correctly on sight.

## Why Spanish pronunciation is easier than English

Spanish is almost entirely phonetic: once you learn the rules, a written word tells you exactly how to say it. There are no silent letters to memorize case-by-case (with a couple of small exceptions below), and vowels never change sound the way English vowels do.

## The five vowels (always the same sound)

| Letter | Pronunciation guide | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a | "ah" as in father | casa (house) |
| e | "eh" as in bed | mesa (table) |
| i | "ee" as in see | sí (yes) |
| o | "oh" as in go | pollo (chicken) |
| u | "oo" as in food | luna (moon) |

Unlike English, these never turn into a "schwa" or glide into another sound — "a" is always "ah", everywhere, every time.

## Tricky consonants

| Letter | Pronunciation guide | English |
|---|---|---|
| ñ | "ny" as in canyon | mañana — tomorrow |
| rr / initial r | rolled/trilled r | perro — dog |
| single r (mid-word) | soft tap, like American "tt" in butter | pero — but |
| j | harsh "h", from the throat | jamón — ham |
| g (before e/i) | same harsh "h" sound as j | gente — people |
| g (before a/o/u) | hard "g" as in go | gato — cat |
| ll | "y" sound (varies by region, sometimes "j" in Argentina) | llamar — to call |
| y | "y" sound, or "ee" when alone | yo — I |
| b / v | identical soft "b" sound — no hard English "v" exists | vaca / barco |
| h | always silent | hola — hello |
| qu | "k" sound, u is silent | qué — what |
| c (before e/i) | soft "s" (Latin America) or "th" (Spain) | cielo — sky |
| c (before a/o/u) | hard "k" | casa — house |

## Stress and the accent mark

Spanish has simple stress rules:
- Words ending in **n, s, or a vowel** → stress the **second-to-last** syllable (ha-BLAN, ca-SA).
- Words ending in **any other consonant** → stress the **last** syllable (ha-BLAR, re-LOJ).
- Any word that breaks these rules carries a written accent mark (´) on the stressed vowel: **café**, **rápido**, **teléfono**.
- The accent mark is also used to distinguish otherwise-identical words: **tú** (you) vs **tu** (your), **sí** (yes) vs **si** (if).

## Practice

1. Read all 8 practice words above out loud, slowly, then at normal speed.
2. Record yourself saying "ferrocarril" and "guitarra" — these combine two of the hardest sounds (rolled r, soft g/j).
3. Pick 5 random English words with "v" in them, say the Spanish-style "b" sound instead, and notice how natural it feels after a few tries.
4. Write down 3 new words you encounter this week and mark where the stress falls using the rules above, before checking Forvo.
